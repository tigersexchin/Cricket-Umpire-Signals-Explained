Cricket Umpire Signals Explained: A Complete Guide to On-Field Decisions

Discover what every important cricket umpire signal means. From boundaries and dismissals to wides, no-balls and TV reviews, understand the gestures that help officials communicate during a match.

✦ Cricket Guide✦ Beginner Friendly✦ Tigerexch

Introduction

Cricket is a game of skill, teamwork and quick decisions. While players attract most of the attention, umpires play an equally important role in keeping every match fair and organised.

During a game, umpires use specific hand and body movements to communicate decisions. These movements are known as cricket umpire signals. They help players, scorers, commentators and spectators understand what has happened on the field.

If you are new to cricket or want to understand the game better, learning these signals is a great place to start. This guide explains the most common umpire signals and what each one means.

Quick Answer: What Are Cricket Umpire Signals?

Cricket umpire signals are standard gestures used by officials to communicate decisions during a match. For example, raising one arm horizontally means a wide ball, raising both arms above the head signals a six, and raising one index finger indicates that a batter is out. These signals make communication clear, especially in a busy stadium where verbal announcements may not be easy to hear.

10 Important Cricket Umpire Signals and Their Meanings

Every umpire signal has a specific purpose. Understanding these gestures makes it easier to follow the action and recognise important decisions during a cricket match.

01

Four Runs Signal

When a batter hits the ball and it reaches the boundary after touching the ground, the batting team receives four runs.

Umpire's signal:
The umpire moves one arm from side to side across the chest.

Why it matters: This signal tells everyone that the batting team has scored four runs from the delivery.

02

Six Runs Signal

A six is scored when the batter hits the ball beyond the boundary without it touching the ground inside the field.

Umpire's signal:
The umpire raises both arms straight above the head.

A six is one of the most exciting moments in limited-overs cricket, and this signal makes the decision easy to recognise.

03

Wide Ball Signal

A wide is called when the delivery is too far from the batter to be reached with a normal cricket shot, according to the applicable playing conditions.

Umpire's signal:
The umpire stretches both arms horizontally out to the sides.

A wide delivery gives the batting team an extra run, and the ball is not counted as one of the legal deliveries in the over.

04

No-Ball Signal

A no-ball is an illegal delivery under the rules of cricket. It can happen for different reasons, such as incorrect foot placement by the bowler or certain illegal bowling actions.

Umpire's signal:
The umpire extends one arm horizontally to the side.

The batting team receives an extra run for a no-ball, and the delivery does not count as a legal ball in the over. The exact consequences can depend on the type of no-ball and the competition's playing conditions.

05

Out Signal

When the umpire decides that a batter is dismissed, the decision is communicated clearly.

Umpire's signal:
The umpire raises one index finger above the head.

This is one of the easiest cricket signals to recognise. It may follow a dismissal through a catch, bowled, leg before wicket (LBW), run out or another recognised method.

06

Dead Ball Signal

A dead ball means the ball is no longer in play. This can happen in several situations, such as when an incident interrupts the delivery or the ball becomes dead under the Laws of Cricket.

Umpire's signal:
The umpire crosses and uncrosses both wrists below the waist.

This signal helps players and officials understand that the current delivery is no longer active.

07

New Ball Signal

In longer formats of cricket, a fielding team may be allowed to take a new ball after a specified number of overs.

Umpire's signal:
The umpire holds the ball above the head.

The availability and timing of a new ball depend on the playing conditions of the match.

08

Short Run Signal

A short run occurs when a batter fails to make good their ground while completing a run, resulting in that run not being counted.

Umpire's signal:
The umpire taps one shoulder with the fingers of the opposite hand.

This signal informs the scorers that a run has not been completed correctly.

09

Free Hit Signal

In certain limited-overs formats, a no-ball can result in a free hit on the next delivery, subject to the competition's rules.

Umpire's signal:
The umpire moves one arm in a circular motion above the head.

During a free hit, the batter has additional protection from certain forms of dismissal. The precise rules depend on the playing conditions.

10

TV Review Signal

When a decision is referred to the television umpire, the on-field umpire communicates the review request.

Umpire's signal:
The umpire makes a rectangle shape with both hands, similar to a television screen.

This signal is associated with the Decision Review System (DRS), where available. The third umpire checks the available technology and sends the decision back to the officials on the field.

Why Are Umpire Signals Important in Cricket?

Umpire signals are more than simple hand movements. They help the entire match run smoothly.

Here are some important reasons:

  1. Clear communication: Players, scorers and spectators can understand decisions without relying only on spoken announcements.
  2. Accurate scoring: Signals help scorers record runs, extras and dismissals correctly.
  3. Better match management: Standard gestures reduce confusion during fast-moving situations.
  4. Support for spectators: People watching from the stands or on television can follow important decisions more easily.
  5. Consistency: Standard signals help officials communicate decisions in a familiar way across different matches.

How Can Beginners Learn Cricket Umpire Signals?

You do not need to memorise every signal at once. Start with the gestures that appear most frequently during a match.

Simple Learning Tips

  • Learn the signals for four, six, wide and no-ball first.
  • Watch how umpires communicate after each delivery.
  • Pay attention to the scoreboard and match commentary.
  • Practise recognising each gesture while watching a cricket match.
  • Read the Laws of Cricket to understand why a decision is made.

With regular practice, these signals become easy to recognise. You will gradually understand how different decisions affect the score and the progress of a match.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

Find quick answers to common questions about cricket umpire signals and their meanings.

1. What does an umpire raising both arms mean?

It means the batter has scored six runs by hitting the ball over the boundary without the ball touching the ground inside the field.

2. What is the signal for a wide ball?

The umpire stretches both arms horizontally to the sides to indicate a wide delivery.

3. How does an umpire signal a no-ball?

The umpire extends one arm horizontally to the side.

4. What does one finger raised by an umpire mean?

It means the batter has been given out.

5. What is the difference between a wide and a no-ball?

A wide is called when a delivery is too far from the batter to be played with a normal shot. A no-ball is an illegal delivery under the Laws of Cricket or the relevant playing conditions. Both can give the batting team an extra run.

6. What is the signal for a dead ball?

The umpire crosses and uncrosses both wrists below the waist.

7. What does the TV review signal mean?

The umpire forms a rectangle with both hands to indicate that a decision is being referred for a television review.

8. Are cricket umpire signals the same in every format?

Most standard signals are common across Test, ODI and T20 cricket. However, some playing conditions and procedures may differ between formats and competitions.

Conclusion

Cricket umpire signals help make the game easier to follow and understand. Whether it is a boundary, a wide delivery, a dismissal or a review, each gesture communicates an important decision.

Learning these signals is useful for new fans as well as regular cricket followers. The next time you watch a match, pay attention to the umpire's movements—you may understand many moments that you previously missed.
